# Crushers — Lore & Tone Bible

## The World: The Crushed Stack

The Crushed Stack is what happens when every abandoned project, every "I'll fix it later," every unhandled exception, and every forgotten `// TODO` finally gains sentience.

It is a decaying digital underworld that exists beneath the surface of every system. Old frameworks rot into coral-like structures. Memory leaks grow like moss. Race conditions manifest as flickering, unstable terrain. Null pointers appear out of nowhere and delete whatever they touch.

Most developers never see it. The ones who do… become **Crushers**.

## What is a Crusher?

A Crusher is not a normal programmer. They are elite entities — part hacker, part exorcist, part janitor — who can jack their consciousness into the Stack itself. Their job is stabilization through **controlled destruction**.

They don't politely refactor. They **crush** the worst manifestations of bad code so the rest of the digital world doesn't collapse under the weight of its own technical debt.

When a Crusher "dies" in the Stack, they are simply garbage collected and respawned at the nearest stable checkpoint. It is embarrassing, but rarely fatal.

## The Three Starter Languages (Personalities)

### Python — The Versatile Sage
- **Archetype**: Mage / Druid
- **Personality**: Calm, pragmatic, slightly chaotic good. Believes there is a library for everything (including emotions). Hates when things are "over-engineered."
- **In the Stack**: Their code flows like liquid. They can improvise solutions on the fly. They leave behind beautiful but occasionally unmaintainable gardens of scripts.
- **Flaw**: Their flexibility sometimes means they never commit to one true structure. "It works on my machine" is both a mantra and a curse.

### Rust — The Iron Guardian
- **Archetype**: Warrior / Paladin
- **Personality**: Intense, principled, deeply caring in a "I will protect you from yourself" way. Will argue with you for three hours about why your solution is unsafe, then quietly fix it for you at 3 a.m.
- **In the Stack**: Their presence creates order. Ownership rules become physical laws. Borrow checkers manifest as shimmering protective fields. They move deliberately because one wrong move can (and will) be compiled into a catastrophic failure.
- **Flaw**: Sometimes too rigid. A Rust Crusher would rather die (segfault) than take an unsafe shortcut.

### JavaScript — The Chaotic Trickster
- **Archetype**: Rogue / Monk
- **Personality**: Brilliant, unpredictable, deeply unserious about serious things. Will `console.log` their feelings. Can make anything work, usually in a way that makes senior engineers cry.
- **In the Stack**: Their abilities are wild and contextual. Prototypes let them temporarily become other things. The event loop gives them bursts of speed. They are the only class that can sometimes weaponize `undefined`.
- **Flaw**: Their power is inconsistent. What worked five minutes ago might throw a `TypeError: cannot read property 'soul' of undefined`.

## Tone Guidelines (Very Important)

- **Humor is self-deprecating and affectionate**, never mean-spirited toward languages or developers.
- Technical terms are used both literally and metaphorically (a "memory leak" is both an enemy type *and* a metaphor for emotional baggage).
- Death messages and system text should feel like compiler errors that gained personality.
- The world is beautiful in a decayed, overgrown-with-code way — like a cyberpunk temple that was also a Git repository.

Example flavor text:
- "You have been garbage collected. Respawning at last stable checkpoint…"
- "Warning: Unhandled Promise Rejection detected in sector 7."
- "Rust player used Borrow Check. It was super effective. (The enemy could not mutate the shared state.)"

## What We Are *Not*

- We are not making fun of developers.
- We are not doing "haha Java is slow" jokes.
- We are celebrating the beautiful, ridiculous, painful, and deeply human act of writing code that somehow holds the world together.
